Privacy and data handling

Local preview · Updated September 26, 2026

This preview reads page text in your browser to explain job requirements. Optional company research is off until you enable it. There are no analytics, ads, accounts, or AI calls in this build.

Automatic page access

The extension has access to HTTP and HTTPS pages to recognize jobs automatically, including links from different sources. It inspects visible page text and job metadata in the top-level document. Standard form values, editable answers, file uploads, and hidden content are excluded. Page text can itself contain personal information; exclusion rules are not a guarantee about everything a website displays.

Local scan text and findings stay in tab memory and are replaced as you navigate or rescan. They are not automatically sent to the developer or saved as a browsing history. You can pause scanning everywhere or disable a site. Chrome’s own site-access controls can further restrict access.

Saved settings and optional reports

Chrome local storage saves preferences, disabled site names, research opt-in, and whether onboarding was dismissed. If you explicitly download an incorrect-result report, it includes role details, findings, quotations, scan time, and the page address with query and fragment removed. It excludes the full extraction and form answers. Reports stay wherever you save them; nothing is automatically submitted.

When you enable company research

The extension sends the employer, title, role ID, location, and page URL without query or fragment to a service running on this computer at http://127.0.0.1:4318. URL paths are retained and may contain identifiers. It does not send the full extracted page text, answers, uploaded documents, or resumes.

The service fetches public official sources. Those websites receive ordinary network requests from your computer. If you configure Brave Search, that provider receives queries containing employer and role details and uses its own privacy terms. Credentials stay in the service. The distributed preview has no hosted developer backend.

Research caching and deletion

The extension keeps up to 50 research entries in Chrome session storage. Turning research off clears that extension cache and hides results. Pending service requests may finish, but the disabled client discards their results.

The separate service saves limited role metadata and public-source excerpts in .research-cache, or its configured cache directory. Ordinary results are reused for 24 hours; unavailable results for one minute. The cache is bounded to approximately 200 entries. Expired files are removed during later lookups, so expiry is not a scheduled deletion guarantee.

Turning research off or uninstalling the extension does not erase the service cache or downloaded reports. Stop the service and delete its cache directory to remove those research files; delete reports separately. Uninstalling removes the extension’s stored preferences. Provider and source-site records are governed by those services.

Use of information

This build uses the described information only for job analysis, requested research, settings, and reports you choose to download. It contains no sale, advertising, analytics, or unrelated transfer mechanism. Future changes to data handling require updated disclosures.
